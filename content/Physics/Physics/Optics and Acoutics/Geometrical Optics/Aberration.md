**Spherical aberration** occurs when light from a point object on the optical axis is more strongly refracted at the periphery OR near the edge of the lens. Spherical aberration results from a wide aperture because the lens refracts outer rays more than inner rays. To ensure sharp images, the aperture is narrowed to stop outer rays reaching the film.
**Chromatic aberration** is a failure of a lens to focus all colors to the same focal point. It occurs because lenses have a different refractive index for different wavelengths of light . The refractive index decreases with increasing wavelength – so violet/blue, having smallest wavelength will be refracted more and red with highest wavelength will be refracted less.
