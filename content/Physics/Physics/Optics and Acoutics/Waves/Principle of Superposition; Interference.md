#waves
Interference refers to what happens when two waves pass through the same region of space at the same time. Consider, for example, the two wave pulses on a string travelling toward each other as shown below.In both cases, the waves meet and pass right by each other. However, in the region where they overlap, the resultant is the displacement is **the algebraic sum of their separate displacements**. This is called the principle of superposition. 
- **Destructive interference**:(A) the two waves have oppposite displacements at the instant they pass one another, they add to zero. 
- **Constructive interference**: (B) at the instant the two pulses overlap, they produce a resultant displacement that is greater than the displacement of either separate pulse.
